Privacy Policy — Paisa Decoder

Last updated: 21 September 2026

Paisa Decoder helps you count the cash you carry abroad, work out which notes to hand over, and check your change. This policy explains exactly what happens to your information when you use it.

Who we are

Paisa Decoder is published by Rashmee and Lokesh. If you have any question about this policy or your information, email assistlinktechnologies@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Your wallet, your spending and your profile details stay on your phone — unless you choose to create an account, which backs up your wallet and spending to it.
  • When you scan one note or coin, it is read on your phone and nothing is sent anywhere. When you photograph a handful of money or a price tag, that photo is sent to Groq, an AI service, to be read. Our reading service also counts those scans against a random app ID, so that no one phone can use up everyone’s scans.
  • We use no analytics and no location tracking, and we never sell or rent your information.
  • The app does not currently show ads. If that changes, this policy will change first.

What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored on your device:

  • the notes and coins you count, and the currencies you carry;
  • your payments — what you bought, the amount, the category, the place you typed, and the time;
  • your trip and any exchange rates you set yourself;
  • your email address, phone number and emergency contact, if you add them;
  • the photos you take of your own notes so the app can show them instead of drawings.

None of this is uploaded unless you create an account, as described below — and even then, your profile details, emergency contact and note photos stay on your phone.

If you create an account

Creating an account is optional. Everything in the app works without one. If you choose to sign in:

Signing in. You can sign in with your phone number, your Google account or, on iPhone, your Apple ID. Sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication, a service provided by Google LLC. It receives what is needed to sign you in — your phone number, which it verifies with a text message, or the name, email address and profile picture your Google or Apple account shares — and keeps a record of your account and when you sign in.

Backup. While you are signed in, the app backs up your wallet, the notes and coins you counted, your payments, your trip, your currency settings and any exchange rates you set, so they are available if you sign in on another phone. The backup is stored with Cloud Firestore, also provided by Google LLC, and only your own account can read it.

Deleting your account. In the app, open Profile and tap Delete account: your account and its backup are deleted from our servers straight away. If you no longer have the app, email assistlinktechnologies@gmail.com from the address or phone number linked to the account and we will delete both. Signing out, by contrast, stops further backups and leaves your information on your phone.

What leaves your phone, and why

Photos you scan. A single note or coin held up to the camera is read by a model on your phone; that picture never leaves the device. When you photograph a handful of notes and coins, or a price tag, the photo is sent over an encrypted connection to our reading service, which runs with a cloud hosting provider. The service passes the photo to Groq, Inc. (United States) to read the currency and amounts, together with the currency you told the app to expect. The result comes back to your phone. We do not store the photos; our service keeps only brief technical records, such as how many pieces were read and whether a reading failed, which do not contain the photos. Groq processes the photos under its own privacy policy, published at groq.com.

A random app ID, to keep scanning fair. The number of scans the app can do each day is limited and shared by everyone who uses it. So that one phone can’t use up everyone’s scans, each scan carries a random ID that the app creates for itself. It isn’t linked to you, your account or your phone’s hardware. Versions of the app from before this change send no ID, so for those our reading service uses the internet address the scan came from instead. Either one is scrambled with a secret key before it is used, and is used only to count scans. Those counts are deleted within about a day.

Your voice. If you say a price or count your cash out loud, your phone's own speech recognition service turns it into text — Google's on Android, Apple's on iPhone. Those services may process the audio on their servers under their own privacy policies. The app receives only the text.

Exchange rates. The app downloads current exchange rates from ExchangeRate-API (open.er-api.com). That request contains no personal information, although, as with any website, the service can see your device's internet address.

Things you choose to share. If you share a summary or export your spending as a PDF or spreadsheet, the file goes only where you send it.

Advertising

The app does not currently show ads, and no advertising company receives any information from it. If we add advertising in future, we will first update this policy to name the advertising partner and describe what it collects — which typically includes your device's advertising identifier — and we will tell you in the app before any ads appear.

Permissions

  • Camera — to scan or photograph notes, coins and price tags.
  • Microphone and speech recognition — to let you say a price or count your cash aloud.

Each permission is asked for only when you first use that feature. If you decline, you can still type prices and tap in your notes and coins.

What we don't do

  • We don't use analytics or tracking tools.
  • We don't collect your location.
  • We don't sell, rent or trade your information.

How long information is kept

Information on your phone stays there until you delete it in the app or uninstall the app, which removes it all. If you have an account, your account and backup are kept until you ask us to delete them. Photos sent for reading are not stored by us; Groq's handling of the photos it processes is set out in its own privacy policy. Scan counts, kept against the scrambled app ID or internet address, are deleted within about a day.

Your choices and rights

You can view, change or delete everything you have entered, at any time, inside the app. Uninstalling the app removes all of it from your phone.

If you are in India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives you rights over your personal data, including to ask how it is processed, to have it corrected or erased, to withdraw your consent, and to raise a complaint. Without an account, almost everything is held only on your device, where you already control it. With an account, we can also correct or delete your backup. For any request or complaint, email assistlinktechnologies@gmail.com and we will respond.

Children

Paisa Decoder is meant for adults travelling abroad and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles your information — including adding advertising — we will update this page and the date at the top, and tell you in the app before any change that affects what leaves your phone.
